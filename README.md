As part of my master thesis I developed and tested a Targeted Memory Reactivation (TMR) Flashcard App. TMR cues neutral association stimuli during learning and represents these stimuli during sleep to boost the natural memory consolidation process. 
Here you can find a customizable version of the study app, that is completely open for anyone who wants to try out TMR at home. If you want to find out more about the thesis study, look here: https://github.com/HML-uos/Master-Thesis-Smartphone-Application-for-Auditory-Targeted-Memory-Reactivation

# What is TMR? 

Targeted Memory Reactivation (TMR) is a neuropsychological technique used to enhance memory consolidation. Neuroscientifically based on the Two-Stage Memory System Model it aims to increase natural, neural reactivation of newly encoded memories in short-term storage sites in the hippocampus (HC) during sleep-dependent consolidation. As HC activations determine neural representations in neocortical long-term storage sites during the consolidation process, this in turn boosts the (neural) strength of consolidated (long-term) memories. To achieve this, TMR connects specific memories with cue stimuli during the encoding process, with olfaction as well as audition as prime stimulus modalities chosen in the majority of TMR studies. The same cue stimuli are then represented during sleep. In turn, the consolidation process is boosted and memory performance increases.

# How does the app work? 

The app allows users to experiment on TMR either with cued flashcards or ambient background sounds. You can download the app as a .apk file via the release in this repository.
**The cued flashcard concept was tested in the thesis and, I have to state this openly, did not work out. The app is therefore only presented for personal experimentation with TMR and holds no claim to actually boost memory performance!**

# Flashcards 

Get started by clicking on the plus button and create your first deck. The app will randomly match sounds to each card. While you learn these sounds will play when flipping a card and when you go to sleep you can replay them directly via the app. You can reassign sounds as often as you like - currently 200 sounds, mostly based on notification and UI sounds, are available. It is currently not definitively clear, how matching the same cue sound to multiple learning experiences effects TMR. If you want your sounds to be unique to each flashcard, you can use the option as shown below.

**You can run own TMR experiment at home!** Use the "Half Replay Mode" function to only recue half of all sounds at night. Learn with the flashcards, use the replay at night and track your learning process. After a few days uncover which cards were replayed in the app and check out whether you performed better or worse at those cards.

**Your first night with the app**

Be warned: users in the study showed significantly worse sleep quality on the first night with the app. Finding the right volume for the nightly replay is a tricky task. Instead of diving right into learning with the app, I'd advise to perform a calibration night with the deck created from the usage tip before. This way you can find your ideal volume level without tainting an actual learning experience. 

Once you are ready to go to sleep you can activate the replay via the moon button for the deck. A submenu will open in which you can either start the replay directly or engage in a volume check. Start with a volume check, which will play 6 unrelated sounds immedíately. Lay down with your phone next to you and use the volume check to gauge a good starting volume for your replay, after the check your volume will be saved. Trust your gut and set the volume so that you can still hear the sounds. 

Afterwards start the replay and get ready to fall asleep. The sounds will play after a 30 minute buffer period, which you can extend as you like, so that you can fall alseep in peace. You can always pause the replay, should the sounds wake you up. If they do, pause and lower the volume a bit. Repeat till you have found a suitable volume level. Any volume changes you make will be saved, so that after one night "calibrating" you should be ready to start your own TMR flashcard experimentation!

# Ambient TMR

Not all learning materials fit into flashcards, the ambient TMR functionality gives you the option to expand your experimentation to a wide range of real life learning scenarios. Simply select one of the three ambiences and play it while you learn. When going to sleep you can replay the ambience with the same buffer and pause functionalities.
